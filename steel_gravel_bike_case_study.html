Independent Study

Fabricating a custom-fit steel frame from raw tubes to complete bicycle

An independent study built around learning the full fabrication process of a bicycle by designing and building a frame fit to my own body and riding style. Practiced skills including machining, TIG welding, tube bending, notching, and fixturing.

01

The Problem

Stock frame geometries are built around averages, not individuals. The goal of this project was to design and hand-build a frame fit to a specific rider and riding style, while using the build as a structured way to develop hands-on fabrication skills — welding, machining, tube prep, and fixturing.

02

Approach

Every stage of the build, from raw tube to finished frame, was done by hand, which meant precision had to come from process and fixturing rather than machine automation.

MACHINING Machined tube ends and fixtures to the tolerances needed for consistent joint fit-up before any welding began.
FORMING Used tube bending and notching to hit the custom geometry and rider fit
WELDING TIG welded the full frame, sequencing joints deliberately to manage heat input and control frame alignment through the weld.

Outcome

Completed a fully rideable, custom-fit steel frame from raw materials. Further building my foundation of direct, hands-on experience in machining, tube forming, and TIG welding that further influences how I approach manufacturability in every design I do.
